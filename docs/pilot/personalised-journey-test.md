# First-user test: a personalised web-app learning path

Use a fresh browser profile or the app's device-reset control. Do not clear a participant's records without their request. A public repository is optional; use a repository the participant is comfortable displaying. Do not request tokens, private source, credentials or personal documents.

## Neutral task

“Imagine you have built a web app with AI and want to understand it better. Use GroundTruth to decide what to practise next, try the task, and show me what the platform now knows about your skills.”

Let the participant navigate before offering help. Record the build, device/viewport, chosen goal, task start/end, first action, points of confusion and assistance supplied. Keep participant notes outside this public repository. Do not turn a positive usability response into evidence of learning gains.

## Observe the first journey

1. Can the learner find the first action and choose a goal without an explanation from the moderator?
2. Can they submit a baseline answer, including “Not sure,” and interpret the feedback without feeling blocked or graded?
3. Can they explain why the next mission was recommended, choose another, and complete a checkpoint after a wrong answer?
4. Can they find the skill map and tell the difference between a baseline response, guided practice, and technology found in a project? Ask: “Does this screen prove you can use this skill independently?”
5. Can they open the live playground and understand its sign-in/pilot-code requirement? Do not provide conceptual assistance during independent phases.
6. Can they find a concept in the reference shelf and return to practice? Ask which languages have practice and which have reference notes only.

## Optional project and field brief

- Enter an explicitly chosen **public** GitHub URL. Show the source-reading notice, choose one or two files, inspect a signal and its file/commit provenance, then follow the practice link. Ask whether the learner thinks GroundTruth has executed or security-audited the app; correct that interpretation after observing it.
- Open Radar. Ask the learner to identify the original publisher/date and separate the headline from the learning suggestion. Test a failed feed without fabricating sample “latest” stories.
- The daily pulse is optional. A signed-in user can answer/change their own vote; fewer than five responses show the total without option counts. Ask what population the poll describes. It is not a representative survey of a profession or job market.

## Data and account checks

Test genuine native sign-in separately from guest usability. Check explicit account-save consent, save/reload, unsaved changes, stale-tab conflict, separate identities, export and deletion. Use synthetic test records for verification. Do not substitute spoofed headers for a hosted identity test.

The learner path expires 30 days after its last explicit save. Poll votes have a 30-day retention window. Operator cleanup is manual, and the existing ten-minute enrollment-security counter exception remains. Private GitHub linking and live Meta requests need separate provisioned authorization/credentials; neither is established by a successful build.

## Your role

Kireeti recruits and moderates with real students, decides how much guidance is acceptable, obtains the intended sharing/recording choices, and judges the student's explanation using the human rubric. The builder can prepare and verify the software, but cannot manufacture real consent, student behavior or learning outcomes.
