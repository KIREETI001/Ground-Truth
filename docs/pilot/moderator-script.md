# Moderator script

Use [consent copy](recruitment-and-consent.md) first and a private [observation sheet](observation-sheet.md). Target 35–45 minutes; durations are planning aids. Record the actual build and timings. Do not disclose a technical answer or patch choice before commitment. Accessibility help is available and recorded separately from technical help.

## Entry and navigation check

For a usability session, begin at Home and ask the participant what they would click first. Let them try the unscored warmup or a concept mission. Observe whether they can find the choice, check the answer, understand feedback, retry, and continue. Concept missions concern general JavaScript reasoning and are separate from the signup investigation; record which missions they attempted before the lab. Their browser-local points are practice completion markers, not assessment results or learning gains. External practice links leave GroundTruth; opening one does not establish completion or certification.

For an investigation-only session, go directly to the Live playground and keep the unaided baseline below before opening technical hints. Record which entry route was used. The floating guide and tutorial may explain controls; any conceptual assistance is logged separately. During independent phases use procedural guidance only. Do not guide the participant through an answer or prior explanation.

## Opening: about 7 minutes

Say: “Please say what you are looking for or expecting when you can. If something is confusing, that is useful information about the product. There is no need to know the vocabulary.”

Before the workbench, ask: “A signup page sends a request to a server that stores a registration. The button is disabled while the request is pending. Sometimes the page does not receive a response. What would you want to find out before deciding whether it is safe to try again? State a prediction and one piece of evidence you would look for.”

Preserve this unaided baseline separately. It is descriptive context, not an equivalent pre-test for calculating gains.

## Main investigation: about 18 minutes

Say: “Use the workbench to decide what this signup app does when a response is discarded and the user tries again. Follow its steps. Before each experiment, save what you expect. Use the requests and persisted rows to support your explanation. You can accept, change, or reject an experiment suggestion; explain your choice.”

Observe prediction, execution, evidence inspection, and explanation. For “Is this right?”, respond: “What evidence would help you decide?” For a confusing control, first ask what they expect it to do. Give a factual label explanation only after they try, and log the help. Never invent evidence to continue a session.

After the revised explanation is saved, say: “Continue to the available repair step. Use the provided choice and check whether the behavior matches your prediction.” Clicking the only vetted patch does not by itself demonstrate understanding.

After verification, ask: “Which observation most changed your explanation, if any?” and “Was there an AI suggestion you accepted, changed, or rejected? What made you decide?” Record instructor-authored fallback or unavailable AI honestly.

## Independent transfer: about 10 minutes

Say: “This is a fresh task. Work alone without AI, hints, or another person's technical help until your final explanation is saved. It is fine to be unsure. We will discuss it afterward. AI stays off for the whole fresh task, including debrief and repair.”

Use only the app's fresh task, displayed code, and request plan. Ask the participant to predict each response and persisted row count with a rationale, commit before executing, inspect actual evidence, and save an independent final explanation. Do not supply expected outcomes, a repair, or outside hints. Record any outside help or leaked answer. An assisted attempt remains useful usability evidence but is not independent transfer.

After final lock, ask: “How did you decide?” Keep that discussion separate from the locked response. The separately labeled repair/debrief may follow. Do not rescore a repaired or debriefed answer as independent work.

## Reflection and close: about 7 minutes

1. “What was hardest to understand or find?”
2. “What made the displayed results believable or doubtful?”
3. “What would you investigate next on your own?”
4. “Where would you expect to use this, if anywhere?”
5. “Was there anything you felt pushed to answer or do?”

Offer removal of accidental identifying information. Show the own export and deletion path if requested. Thank the participant without commenting on ability or claiming a learning gain. Complete the factual observation sheet promptly; keep hypotheses separate from observations.

## Help and stop rules

During a navigation block, allow about 60–90 seconds unless help is requested or the person is distressed. Use H0 no help; H1 neutral task reread; H2 navigation/label help; H3 mechanism or answer disclosed. H3 ends independence for subsequent reasoning. Record time, reason, and content category of each intervention. Accessibility help is recorded separately.

Stop for withdrawal, distress, personal/production data entry, any ownership or secret leak, fabricated evidence, or a blocking defect. Close enrollment and tell the operator. A voluntary stop is not a learner failure. Review issues after the first two participants; continue only after serious defects are fixed and affected checks repeat on the new build.
